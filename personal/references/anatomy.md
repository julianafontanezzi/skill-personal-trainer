# Anatomy for choosing exercises

## Compound vs isolation

- **Compound (multi-joint):** several muscle groups at once, more load, more energy cost. Goes first in the workout (more tension-oriented).
- **Isolation (single-joint):** one group. Goes after (more metabolic).

## Exercises by muscle group

| Group | Compounds | Isolations |
|---|---|---|
| Quadriceps (rectus femoris, vastus medialis, lateralis, intermedius) | squat, leg press, lunge | leg extension |
| Hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) | Romanian deadlift/stiff-leg; also work in squat and leg press | lying leg curl, seated leg curl |
| Glutes | squat, leg press, lunge, deadlift, hip thrust | hip abduction (gluteus medius) |
| Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus) | | standing calf raise (gastrocnemius), seated calf raise (soleus) |
| Pectoralis major (upper, middle, lower portions) | flat, incline, decline bench press, push-up | pec deck, fly |
| Back (latissimus dorsi, trapezius, rhomboids, teres, infraspinatus) | pulldowns and pull-ups (width, "V" shape), rows (thickness) | straight-arm pulldown |
| Deltoid (anterior, lateral, posterior) | overhead press | front raise, lateral raise, bent-over raise/reverse fly |
| Biceps brachii | takes part in pulls and rows | barbell curl |
| Triceps (long, lateral, medial heads) | takes part in bench, push-up and overhead press | cable pushdown, overhead extension, skull crusher |
| Forearm | takes part in anything with a grip | wrist curl |
| Abs (rectus abdominis, obliques) | stabilize in almost everything | crunch variations, plank |

Reminders: the rear deltoid is often forgotten; the triceps is most of the arm (about 60% of arm mass).

## What each muscle does

| Muscle | Main action |
|---|---|
| Pectoralis major | shoulder adduction (bringing the arm toward the body) |
| Rectus abdominis | trunk flexion |
| External oblique | covers the whole side of the abdomen (not just the "waist") |
| Trapezius (upper, middle, lower) | scapular retraction and stabilization; the upper part elevates the shoulder |
| Latissimus dorsi + teres major | shoulder extension, adduction and internal rotation (pulldowns) |
| Serratus anterior | stabilizes the scapula against the ribs |
| Deltoid | shoulder abduction; anterior helps flexion, posterior horizontal abduction |
| Rotator cuff | supraspinatus: abduction; infraspinatus and teres minor: external rotation; subscapularis: internal rotation (teres major is not part of it) |
| Biceps brachii | elbow flexion and forearm supination (inserts on the radius) |
| Brachialis | elbow flexion in any grip (inserts on the ulna) |
| Brachioradialis | helps elbow flexion |
| Triceps brachii | elbow extension; the long head also extends the shoulder |
| Gluteus maximus | main hip extensor (standing up, sitting, walking) |
| Gluteus medius | hip abduction and stabilization |
| Tensor fasciae latae | hip abduction (weaker than gluteus medius) |
| Quadriceps | knee extension |
| Hamstrings (long head of biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) | knee flexion and hip extension; the short head of biceps femoris is not a true hamstring |
| Tibialis anterior | dorsiflexion (front of the lower leg) |
| Fibularis (peroneus) longus and brevis | foot eversion |
| Triceps surae (medial and lateral gastrocnemius + soleus) | plantar flexion |

## Using the action to choose the exercise

- Hip extensors (gluteus maximus and hamstrings): squat, deadlift, hip thrust.
- Curls with a neutral or pronated grip (hammer, reverse) emphasize brachialis and brachioradialis.
- Arm behind the body (overhead triceps extension) stretches and emphasizes the long head of the triceps.
- **Thigh** runs from hip to knee; **lower leg** is from the knee down.
