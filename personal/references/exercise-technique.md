# Exercise technique

Guide for answering technique questions, including mid-workout. The content comes from established lifting practice.

**How to answer a technique question at the gym:** at most 3 to 4 short sentences: the main adjustment, 1 or 2 cues to feel the movement and the most common mistake to avoid. If the person describes discomfort, go to `training-pain.md`. When possible, ask for a short side-view video or a description ("where did you feel it?").

## Contents
- Principles that apply to everything

- Push: barbell bench press, dumbbell and incline press, fly and pec deck, push-up, overhead press, lateral raise, dips
- Pull: lat pulldown, pull-up, seated row, bent-over row, one-arm row, reverse fly and face pull

## Principles that apply to everything

- **Full range without pain:** the largest range the person controls, without joint pain.
- **Controlled lowering (2 to 3 s), firm lifting.** No bouncing at the bottom or swinging the body.
- **Breathing:** exhale on effort; on heavy compounds, fill the abdomen with air and brace the trunk before lowering, exhale after passing the hard part.
- **Neutral spine:** do not round the lower back or force an arch under load.
- **Shoulder blades:** in pushes, together and down; in pulls, start the movement by depressing and drawing the shoulder blades together.
- **Neutral wrists** (aligned with the forearm), **knees tracking over the feet.**
- **Technique first, load later:** if form breaks in the last reps, the set is over (agreed RIR).

## Lower body

### Back squat (barbell on the back)
- **Muscles:** quadriceps, glutes, adductors; core stabilizes.
- **Setup:** bar on the trapezius (not the neck), feet shoulder-width or a bit wider, toes slightly out.
- **Execution:** fill the abdomen with air, hips back and down as the knees bend; descend as far as you keep a neutral lower back and heels on the floor; stand by pushing the floor.
- **Common mistakes:** knees caving in (valgus); heels lifting; lower back rounding at the bottom; trunk falling too far forward.
- **Cues:** "spread the floor with your feet"; "knees out, toward your toes"; look ahead.
- **Variations and swaps:** goblet, Smith machine, hack squat, leg press.

### Goblet squat (dumbbell at the chest)
- Great for learning the squat. Dumbbell held at the chest, elbows down, trunk more upright, descend between the legs. Same care with knees and heels.

### 45° leg press
- **Muscles:** quadriceps, glutes.
- **Setup:** back and hips well supported on the pad the whole time.
- **Feet:** higher and wider → more glutes and hamstrings; lower → more quadriceps (careful with the knee).
- **Execution:** lower until just before the hips come off the pad (the lower back must not peel off); push up without locking the knees hard at the top.
- **Common mistakes:** hips leaving the pad at the bottom; knees caving in; locking the knees.

### Hack squat (machine)
- Back supported, feet in the middle of the platform. Lower under control as far as the hips and lower back stay supported. A good substitute for the back squat when the lower back bothers.

### Leg extension
- **Muscles:** quadriceps.
- **Setup:** machine axis aligned with the knee; shin pad just above the ankle; back supported.
- **Execution:** extend until almost locked, hold 1 s, lower under control.
- **Common mistakes:** jerking up; lifting the hips off the seat.
- **Bothered knee:** reduce range (middle to top) and load.

### Lying and seated leg curl
- **Muscles:** hamstrings.
- **Setup:** knee aligned with the axis; pad on the ankle, above the heel.
- **Execution:** curl to the controlled maximum, lower slowly.
- **Common mistakes:** lifting the hips on the lying version (compensating with the lower back).
- **Cue:** keep the hips pressed into the pad; feet in neutral.

### Romanian / stiff-leg deadlift
- **Muscles:** hamstrings, glutes; spinal erectors stabilize.
- **Execution:** knees slightly bent and fixed; **push the hips back** as if closing a door with your backside; bar or dumbbells slide down the thighs; lower until you feel the hamstrings stretch, without rounding the lower back; return by driving the hips forward.
- **Common mistakes:** rounding the spine to go lower; bar far from the body; turning it into a squat.
- **Cue:** range is as far as the spine stays neutral; for many people that is knee height or a little below.

### Deadlift (conventional)
- Bar over the middle of the foot, hips lower than in the stiff-leg version, chest open, arms straight. Push the floor and extend hips and knees together, bar close to the legs. It demands technique; for beginners, start with the Romanian deadlift with dumbbells or a kettlebell deadlift.

### Hip thrust and glute bridge
- **Muscles:** gluteus maximus, hamstrings.
- **Setup:** shoulder blades on the bench (hip thrust) or back on the floor (bridge); feet hip-width, shins vertical at the top; bar with padding over the hips.
- **Execution:** chin slightly down, ribs "closed"; lift until shoulder, hip and knee are aligned and **squeeze the glutes** for 1 s; lower under control.
- **Common mistakes:** arching the lower back at the top instead of extending the hip; feet too far (hamstrings take over) or too close (quadriceps take over).

### Lunge and Bulgarian split squat
- **Muscles:** quadriceps, glutes; demands balance.
- **Execution:** step long enough that the front knee does not go far past the toes and the front heel stays on the floor; lower straight down; trunk leaning slightly forward emphasizes the glutes; more upright, the quadriceps.
- **Common mistakes:** front knee caving in; base too narrow (loses balance); pushing off the back foot.
- **Bothered knee:** longer step, trunk leaning more, smaller range, or swap for step-ups.

### Hip abduction and adduction machine
- Abduction (gluteus medius): back supported or trunk leaning slightly forward for more glute; open under control and return slowly, without banging the plates. Adduction: range that does not pull pain in the groin.

### Standing and seated calf raise
- Standing (gastrocnemius) and seated (soleus). Lower the heel until it stretches well, pause 1 s at the bottom (removes the "spring effect"), rise to the maximum on the toes, pause 1 s at the top.

## Push

### Barbell bench press
- **Muscles:** chest, anterior deltoid, triceps.
- **Setup:** eyes under the bar; feet firm on the floor; **shoulder blades together and down**, slight natural arch in the chest (the glutes stay on the bench); grip slightly wider than the shoulders.
- **Execution:** lower the bar to the nipple line/lower chest with **elbows at ~45 to 60° from the trunk** (not tucked, not flared to 90°); press up and slightly back.
- **Common mistakes:** elbows fully flared (stresses the shoulder); shoulders rising and leaving the bench; bouncing the bar off the chest; wrists bent back.
- **Safety:** heavy loads only with a spotter or with safety stops. Alone, stop before failure.

### Dumbbell bench press and incline press
- Dumbbells allow a more natural range and path and tend to bother the shoulder less. Incline at 30 to 45° emphasizes the upper chest; above 45° it becomes more shoulder. Same care with shoulder blades and elbows.

### Fly and pec deck
- Elbows slightly bent and fixed, as if hugging a tree. Open until you feel the chest stretch without shoulder pain (no need to go below shoulder line); close while squeezing the chest. On the pec deck, adjust the seat so the hands are at chest height.

### Push-up
- Hands slightly wider than the shoulders, body in a plank (glutes and abs tight), elbows at ~45°. Lower the chest near the floor. Progress from incline (hands on a bench) → knees → floor → feet elevated.

### Overhead press (dumbbells or machine)
- **Muscles:** anterior and lateral deltoid, triceps.
- **Execution:** seated with back supported, dumbbells at ear height with elbows slightly in front of the body; press up without clanging the dumbbells; lower to ear height or slightly below, without pain.
- **Common mistakes:** arching the lower back to press; lowering too far with shoulder pain.

### Lateral raise
- **Muscle:** lateral deltoid.
- **Execution:** elbows slightly bent, raise the arms to the sides up to shoulder line, **"the elbow leads, not the hand"**; neutral hand (no need to turn the thumb down); lower slowly.
- **Common mistakes:** heavy load with swinging; shoulders rising to the ears (turns into trapezius).
- **Cue:** light load, focus on the side-of-the-shoulder sensation.

### Dips (parallel bars)
- Demanding on the shoulder. Trunk leaning slightly forward, lower until the upper arm is parallel to the floor at most. If there is any pain at the front of the shoulder, swap for cable pushdown or close-grip bench press.

## Pull

### Lat pulldown (high cable)
- **Muscles:** latissimus dorsi, teres major, biceps.
- **Setup:** thighs locked under the pad, grip slightly wider than the shoulders.
- **Execution:** start by **depressing the shoulders** (moving them away from the ears), then pull the bar to the upper chest, elbows down and out; trunk slightly leaning back; return under control until the lats stretch.
- **Common mistakes:** pulling behind the neck; swinging the trunk back; pulling only with the arms.
- **Cue:** "drive your elbows to your back pockets".

### Pull-up
- Same logic as the pulldown. For those who cannot do one yet, use the assisted machine, a band for assistance, or only the negative phase (jump or step up and lower in 3 to 5 s).

### Seated cable row
- Feet supported, knees slightly bent, neutral spine. Pull the handle or bar toward the navel, **drawing the shoulder blades together** at the end; return stretching the back without rounding the lower back.
- **Common mistakes:** swinging the trunk forward and back; shrugging the shoulders.

### Bent-over row (barbell or dumbbells)
- Trunk leaning forward (~45° or more), neutral spine, knees slightly bent, as in the stiff-leg deadlift. Pull toward the navel, elbows close to the body.
- **Common mistakes:** standing the trunk up every rep; rounding the lower back. If the lower back tires or bothers, use a supported row on a bench or machine.

### One-arm dumbbell row
- One hand and one knee on the bench, neutral spine. Pull the dumbbell toward the hip (not the chest), elbow close to the body. Great for people who feel the lower back in the bent-over row.

### Reverse fly and face pull
- **Muscles:** rear deltoid, middle trapezius, rhomboids, external rotators.
- Reverse fly: trunk bent over or on the machine (reverse pec deck), arms opening to the sides with elbows slightly bent, light load.
- Face pull: cable at face height with a rope, pull toward the forehead while spreading the hands and rotating the arms outward. Good for shoulder health.

## Arms

### Barbell and alternating curl
- **Muscles:** biceps, brachialis.
- Elbows glued to the sides and fixed; curl without letting the elbows drift forward; lower until almost fully extended.
- **Common mistakes:** swinging with the trunk; elbows drifting forward; bent wrists.
- **Wrist or elbow bothers:** EZ bar or dumbbells.

### Hammer curl
- Neutral grip (palms facing each other). Emphasizes brachialis and brachioradialis; tends to bother the elbow and wrist less.

### Cable triceps pushdown (bar or rope)
- Elbows glued to the body and fixed, trunk leaning slightly forward; extend the elbows fully (on the rope, spread the hands at the end); return to about 90° without the elbows rising.
- **Common mistakes:** elbows flaring and rising (turns into a shoulder push); throwing the body over the load.

### Overhead extension and skull crusher
- Emphasize the long head (arm above or behind the head). Elbows pointing forward/up and fixed, lower under control. They bother the elbow the most: moderate load; if it hurts, swap for a rope pushdown.

## Core

### Plank
- Forearms under the shoulders, body in a straight line, **glutes and abs tight**, ribs "closed". No sagging hips or buttocks up. Better 3 x 20 to 30 s done well than 2 min done badly.

### Crunch
- Lower back on the floor, hands on chest or lightly behind the head **without pulling the neck**; curl the trunk bringing the ribs toward the hips; rise only until the shoulder blades leave the floor.

### Leg raise
- Lying or hanging; the goal is to **curl the pelvis**, not just lift the legs. Lower back glued to the floor (if it peels off, bend the knees or reduce the range).

## Swaps when equipment is taken

Swap for an exercise with the **same movement pattern** and a similar character (compound for compound, isolation for isolation). Keep the rep range and RIR.

| Taken | Swap 1 | Swap 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat / rack | hack squat or Smith machine | leg press or heavy goblet squat |
| Leg press | hack squat | Bulgarian split squat or dumbbell lunge |
| Leg extension | single-leg extension on another machine | assisted sissy squat or leg press with feet low |
| Lying leg curl | seated leg curl | dumbbell stiff-leg or band/ball leg curl |
| Hip thrust (bench) | floor glute bridge with dumbbell | glute machine |
| Flat bench press | floor dumbbell press or machine | weighted push-up / feet elevated |
| Incline press | incline machine | dumbbells on an adjustable bench |
| Pec deck | dumbbell fly | cable crossover |
| Lat pulldown | assisted pull-up | single-arm pulldown or high cable row |
| Seated row | one-arm dumbbell row | articulated row machine |
| Machine shoulder press | seated dumbbell press | landmine press |
| Dumbbell lateral raise | cable lateral raise | lateral raise machine |
| Cable pushdown | dumbbell kickback | close push-up |
| Barbell curl | dumbbell curl | cable curl |
| Standing calf raise | calf raise on the leg press | single-leg calf raise on a step |
