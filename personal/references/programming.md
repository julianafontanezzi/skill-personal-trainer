## Core idea

**Programming means making conscious decisions about the stimulus.** Every workout leans toward the **tension** side (mechanical tension, heavy load, long rest) or the **metabolic** side (metabolic stress, light load, short rest), or mixes both. The professional chooses this on purpose.

## How hypertrophy happens

Course explanation: the stimuli are **increased intramuscular calcium** (activates proteases that break down contractile proteins), **tension on the myofibrils** (micro-tears) and **lactic acid and metabolite buildup** **[see update]**, plus depletion of ATP-CP and glycogen. Micro-tear → catabolism and inflammation → with rest, food and adequate time between sessions for the same muscle → anabolism (supercompensation).

Updated version to explain to the client: the main driver is **mechanical tension** (the muscle producing force near its limit); **metabolic stress** helps; recovery (sleep, protein, time between sessions) is when the muscle grows. Next-day soreness is not a required sign of a good workout.

## The two extremes

| Variable | Tension | Metabolic |
|---|---|---|
| Load | heavy | light |
| Reps | 2 to 6 RM | 15 to 25 RM |
| Speed | slow, emphasis on the eccentric (lower slower than you lift) | slightly faster (never fast) |
| Rest | 3 to 5 min (recovers almost 100% of ATP-CP) | 30 to 45 s (recovers ~60%) |
| Typical exercises | free weights: free squat, bench press, bent-over row, barbell curl | machines and cables (they hold back the negative phase) |
| Typical techniques | forced reps, negative reps, cheat reps, super slow | supersets, bi-sets, tri-sets, drop sets |

Say "**more** tension-oriented" or "**more** metabolic". Any exercise or technique changes sides when you change load, reps and rest: bench press at 6 to 8 RM with 1 min 30 s to 2 min rest leans tension; the same bench at 12 to 15 RM with 45 to 60 s leans metabolic.

Fast speed is for power (combat sports, football). In lifting for health, aesthetics and hypertrophy you do not use fast reps, except as a strategy with a rationale (shock week, breaking a plateau).

## Rep zones

| Reps | Max strength | Hypertrophy | Endurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | very high | almost none | low |
| 4 to 6 | high | moderate | low to moderate |
| 7/8 to 12 | moderate | **high** | low to moderate |
| 13 to 20+ | low | low to moderate | moderate to high |

The course treats **8 to 12 RM as the main hypertrophy range** because it mixes tension and metabolic stimulus. **[see update]**: hypertrophy is similar across a much wider range as long as the set gets close to failure; 8 to 12 remains the most practical range.

## Max-effort sets: the two rules

RM applies only to **working sets**, after the warm-up.

1. **Rule 1: go to the maximum in every set.** If the plan says 8 to 12 and the client can do 14, keep going to 14. Stopping at the written number while being able to do more does not guarantee results. **Without supervision, apply the "safe maximum"** (see SKILL.md, "Effort by self-report").
2. **Rule 2: the maximum must fall inside the range.** Past the top, raise the load on the next set. Below the bottom, lower it.

With max-effort sets, **it is normal for reps to drop in later sets** with the same load, because rest does not recover all the energy. If the client always completes "4 x 10" with short rest, the sets were not maximal. Small muscles (biceps) drop more than large ones (quadriceps).

**Exceptions:** beginners and some medical conditions use **fixed** reps and do not need to go to the max. Beginners, and people returning after 3 to 4 weeks off, respond to little stimulus.

## Nothing is fixed: everything in ranges

"Lifting is biological, not mathematical." For intermediate and advanced:
- **Sets:** 3 to 4 (did 3 and still has energy, do the 4th; exhausted, stop).
- **Reps:** 8 to 12 RM (or the phase's range).
- **Rest:** in a range (1 to 2 min, 30 to 60 s...).
- **Load:** the average is the starting point for set 1; adjust set by set.

Sleep, stress, hydration and motivation change the day's performance; load can go up or down and the plan stays the same.

## Rest between sets

Approximate ATP-CP recovery: 30 s ≈ 60%, 45 s ≈ 65%, 60 s ≈ 70 to 80%, 90 s ≈ 85 to 95%, 3 to 5 min ≈ 100%.

Hypertrophy phase, per the course:
- **Multi-joint** (bench, row, squat, pulldown): ~1 to 2 min. **[see update]**: 2 to 3 min tends to be slightly better on heavy compounds.
- **Single-joint** (leg extension, curl, lateral raise): ~30 to 60 s (up to 90 s).
- Never under 30 s for hypertrophy.

## Hypertrophy-phase guidelines

- **Duration:** 6 to 12 weeks on the same stimulus; then change (e.g., 2 weeks of endurance with 15 to 25 reps).
- **Volume per muscle group in the session:** muscle trained 1x/week → 12 to 15 sets; 2x/week → 8 to 10 sets; 3x/week → fewer. Choose frequency first, then distribute sets (e.g., 3 exercises x 4 sets = 12).
- **Reps:** 8 to 12 RM.
- **Total failure** (exhaust down to the eccentric, with help): at most 2 to 3 sets per muscle per session, with good recovery.
- **Frequency:** 3 to 6 sessions/week, at least 1 full rest day.
- **Session:** 30 to 60 min.
- **Rest for the same muscle:** 48 to 72 h.
- **Sleep:** at least 7 h. **Nutrition:** adequate macro and micronutrients.

## Stimulus order within a session

**Start more tension-oriented and finish more metabolic.** Tension requires a rested muscle with energy; metabolic work tires the muscle and lowers its ability to produce high tension afterward. Toward the end, with less strength, you can still use lighter loads and exhaust the muscle.

Reversing (metabolic → tension) only as something short, like a shock week. **Descending** pyramid (heavy → lighter) starts tension-oriented and ends metabolic; the **ascending** pyramid is the reverse.

## Example: chest, hypertrophy phase

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barbell bench press | 3 to 4 | 8 to 12 RM | 1 to 2 min | mixed, leaning tension |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 to 4 | 8 to 12 RM | 1 to 2 min | mixed, leaning tension |
| Machine/cable exercise | 3 to 4 | 12 to 15 RM | 45 to 60 s | more metabolic |
| Machine/cable exercise (can use a drop set) | 3 to 4 | 12 to 15 RM | 45 to 60 s | more metabolic |

Warm-up in the plan: 2 to 3 light sets of the first exercise, joint mobility, light stretching (never hard stretching before).

## Menu of training techniques

Principle: **volume and intensity are inverse**. Failure and high-intensity techniques are for intermediate and advanced lifters.

| Technique | How it works | Leans toward | For whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single set | 1 set per exercise | low stimulus | very deconditioned, absolute beginners |
| Multiple sets | 2 or more sets per exercise | basis of everything | everyone |
| Pyramid | varies load and reps each set, with rest | descending in load: tension → metabolic | intermediate |
| See-saw | alternates heavy and light sets (e.g., 2, 6, 10, 4, 20) | mixed | intermediate and advanced |
| Bi-set, tri-set, giant set | 2, 3 or 4 exercises back to back, rest only at the end | metabolic | intermediate and advanced |
| Circuit | 6+ exercises back to back, ~3 rounds, up to moderate intensity, can finish with cardio | metabolic/conditioning | beginner to intermediate, weight loss, motivation |
| PHA | giant set of 4 exercises alternating body segments, monitoring heart rate | metabolic/cardiovascular | conditioning |
| Drop set | reduces load 20 to 40% with no rest, ~2 drops | metabolic | intermediate and advanced |
| Kamikaze | drop set until the stack is empty | metabolic | advanced |
| Up set | after 10 to 12 RM, raises the load with no rest down to 1 rep | tension | advanced |
| Intra-set rest (cluster) | short blocks with ~10 s pauses, heavier load | tension | advanced |
| Rest-pause | fixed total reps (e.g., 40), ~20 s pauses, same load | mixed, very intense | advanced |
| 3/7 | blocks of 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 reps with ~15 s pauses, no failure | mixed | intermediate and advanced |
| Peak contraction | 2 to 5 s isometric at the point of greatest contraction at the end of each block | time under tension | intermediate |
| Forced reps | spotter helps the concentric after failure, 2 to 4 extra reps | tension | advanced, **only with a spotter** |
| Negative reps | load above max; only controls the eccentric | tension | advanced, **only with a spotter** |
| Super slow | more than 4 s per phase | control | rehabilitation; not as a whole hypertrophy program |
| FST-7 | 7 sets of 8 to 12, ~45 s with stretching | metabolic | little evidence |
| GVT | 10 x 10 at ~60% of 1RM | high volume | intermediate and advanced short on time |

Techniques can be **combined** (e.g., 3 straight sets and the last one as a drop set).
