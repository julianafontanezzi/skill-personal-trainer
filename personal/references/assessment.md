# Initial assessment and intake

The form to fill in is `../templates/client-profile.md`.

## Principles

- **Intake (history-taking) is the first and most important part of the assessment.** Tests are simple; the person's history provides the line of reasoning. "The clinic is sovereign": what the person feels and reports weighs more than an isolated number.
- Assessment starts before the conversation: in person, watch how the person walks, whether they limp, facial expressions of pain. Remotely, ask.
- Use the person's language, no jargon. **Do not suggest answers** ("Do you smoke?", not "You don't smoke, right?").
- With a companion, notice whether they interfere or fill in something omitted.
- History gets completed over the following sessions; record everything.

## Outline

1. **Personal data:** name, age, sex, contact, **job and routine** (e.g., sits all day).
2. **Goal and main complaint.**
3. **Current history:** pain or problems now, since when, what makes it worse and better.
4. **Past history:** illnesses, injuries, surgeries, hospitalizations, accidents, old discomforts.
5. **Health:** PAR-Q, medications, diagnoses, exams, blood pressure, medical clearance.
6. **Habits:** current and past physical activity, lifting experience, sleep, food, smoking, alcohol, stress.
7. **Logistics:** days per week, time per session, place (gym, home, building gym), equipment, schedule, preferences and dislikes.
8. **Measurements (if possible):** weight, height, BMI, circumferences (compare sides), posture photos with consent.
9. **Simple functional tests:** range of motion, tightness, bodyweight squat (watch knees, heels, trunk), gait and compensations.

## The four pain questions

1. **Where does it hurt?** If it is "all over", ask where it hurts most today and focus on that.
2. **How much does it hurt?** 0 to 10 scale: 0 to 3 mild, 4 to 7 moderate, 8 to 10 intense. Reapply to track. **The score does not measure the severity of an injury.**
3. **What does it feel like?** Stabbing, burning, shooting, heavy, tingling. The type of pain helps tell muscle tissue from nerve.
4. **When does it hurt?** For how long and in which movements or situations.

## Warning signs (refer before training the area)

- Pain 8 to 10, pain that gets worse with training or does not improve with rest.
- Shooting pain, tingling, numbness, loss of strength.
- Signs of inflammation: **heat, redness, swelling, pain and loss of function**.
- Significant asymmetries: one leg shorter, a hump on the back when bending forward (the sign of the Adams test for scoliosis).
- Night pain that wakes the person, fever with pain, unexplained weight loss.

The personal trainer **does not give a clinical diagnosis**. Work **across disciplines** with doctors, physiotherapists and dietitians and adjust training to what they advise.

## Reassessment

Full reassessment **every 3 months**, with the measurement protocol in `reassessment.md`.
