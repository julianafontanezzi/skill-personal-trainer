# Progression and follow-up

Rules for deciding, at each check-in, what changes in the training. Parts coming from the base lifting course are marked **(course)**; the rest is established strength-training practice, used to fill in what the course does not cover.

## Principle

The body adapts to the stimulus; to keep improving, the stimulus must increase gradually or change. Change **one variable at a time** and only after 1 to 2 weeks of data. Order of preference: technique → reps → load → sets → frequency → intensity method.

## Load progression (double progression)

Works together with the two RM rules **(course)**.
1. Work inside the range (e.g., 8 to 12 RM) at the agreed RIR.
2. When the client **reaches the top of the range** on the first set (or all sets, for beginners), with good form and the agreed RIR, **raise the load** next session.
3. Size of the increase: the smallest available increment, usually **2 to 5% on compounds** (e.g., +2 to 5 kg on squat or leg press, +1 to 2 kg on bench press) and **1 to 2 kg or one plate on isolations**. With heavy dumbbells the jump can be too big: first gain reps above the range.
4. After the increase, reps fall back near the bottom of the range and the cycle restarts.
5. If reps fall below the bottom of the range for 2 sessions, go back to the previous load **(course: rule 2)**.

Beginners progress fast (sometimes every session in the first weeks); advanced lifters slowly (weeks for a small increase). That is normal.

## When to progress volume and frequency

- Start with the **lowest volume that produces progress**. Beginner: 1 to 3 sets per exercise.
- If load and reps stopped rising, recovery is good (sleep, muscle soreness fades within 2 to 3 days) and the client has time, **add 1 to 2 sets per muscle group per week**.
- Course limits per session: 12 to 15 sets for a muscle trained 1x/week; 8 to 10 per session for 2x/week **(course)**.
- More frequency (training a muscle 2x instead of 1x) is a good way to spread volume when the session gets too long (over 60 min).

## Changing phase

- Keep the same stimulus for **6 to 12 weeks** and then change **(course)**. This skill's standard: **full reassessment and a new program every 3 months (12 weeks)**, with an optional variation midway through the quarter (`reassessment.md`). Examples of change:
  - more tension-oriented period: 4 to 6 or 5 to 8 RM, longer rest;
  - endurance or metabolic shock period: 15 to 20 or 15 to 25 reps, short rest, for 1 to 2 weeks **(course)**;
  - exercise swap (variations of the same pattern: flat bench → incline dumbbell press);
  - introducing a technique (drop set on the last set, rest-pause), only for intermediate and advanced lifters.
- For beginners: after 4 to 8 weeks of adaptation, move to the hypertrophy phase (see `beginners-and-splits.md`).
- Reassess when changing phase.

## Deload week

Schedule it or use it when the signs appear.
- **When:** every 4 to 8 weeks of hard training, or when there are 2 or more of these signs: reps falling over 2 sessions in a row, soreness for more than 3 days, bad sleep, tired all day, low motivation, small joint aches.
- **How:** 1 week with **about half the sets**, same technique, same or slightly lower load, **RIR 3 to 4** (nothing near failure). No intense techniques.
- Then return to the plan. Often the client returns stronger.

## Plateau

Plateau = **3 weeks or more with no load or rep progress** on the main exercises, with good adherence.
1. **Check the basics first:** real adherence (did they train what was planned?), sleep (7 h or more), food and protein (refer to a dietitian if needed), stress, technique and whether RIR is really low in the sets.
2. **If the basics are fine and there is fatigue:** deload week.
3. **If there is no fatigue:** change the stimulus, one thing at a time:
   - rep range (e.g., 8 to 12 → 5 to 8 for a few weeks);
   - metabolic shock week **(course)**;
   - variation of the main exercises;
   - +1 to 2 weekly sets for the lagging muscle;
   - an intensity technique (intermediate and advanced).
4. Reassess in 2 to 4 weeks.

## Missed sessions, breaks and return

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Missed 1 or 2 workouts | continue where you left off; do not "make up" by doubling up |
| 1 week off | return with the same workout; if heavy, ~10% less load in the first session |
| 2 to 3 weeks off | 1 week with ~10 to 20% less load and one set fewer, RIR 3; then normal |
| 4 weeks or more off | treat as reintroduction: 1 to 2 weeks with reduced volume and load and RIR 3 to 4. Returners respond fast to little stimulus and get sore easily **(course)** |
| Off because of illness or surgery | medical clearance first; restart as a beginner |
| Adherence below 50% for 2 check-ins | the plan does not fit the routine: reduce days or duration before touching the content |

## Pain during follow-up

Use the guide `training-pain.md`, which has the pain traffic light and the adjustments by region. In short:
- **Delayed-onset soreness** (diffuse, 1 to 3 days later, goes away by itself): normal; if it is strong and frequent, reduce volume.
- **Joint discomfort 3 to 4 that does not worsen:** adjust the exercise (range, grip, variation, lighter load) and monitor.
- **5 or more, or increasing during the set:** remove the exercise and keep training what does not hurt.
- **Does not improve in 1 to 2 weeks, swelling, tingling, loss of strength:** refer to a doctor or physiotherapist.
- Ask for the pain score at every check-in and log it in the profile.

## Reassessment

| What | When |
|---|---|
| Loads and reps, RIR | every session (log) |
| Pain, sleep, adherence, motivation | every check-in (1 to 2 weeks) |
| Average weight and waist | mini-check in week 6 of the quarter |
| Full measurements, photos, tests, goals, new program | **every 3 months** (`reassessment.md`) |
| PAR-Q | every 12 months or when health changes |

Show progress to the client with numbers. It is the best argument for them to keep going.
