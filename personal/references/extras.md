## Warm-up, mobility and stretching

**Warm-up (5 to 10 min):**
1. General: 3 to 5 light min on a bike, treadmill, elliptical or jumping jacks, until warm and breathing a bit faster.
2. Mobility for the joints of the day: shoulder circles, trunk and hip rotation, bodyweight squat, lunge with rotation, ankles.
3. Activation (optional): glute bridge, band to push the knees out (leg day), light face pull or band (upper-body day).
4. Ramp-up sets of the first exercise for each group: for example 1 set with ~50% of the load x 10, 1 set with ~75% x 5, then start.

**Stretching:**
- Before training: light or dynamic only. Long static stretching (over ~60 s per muscle) right before can slightly reduce strength.
- After training or at another time: static 20 to 30 s per muscle, 2 to 3 times, for those who want to gain flexibility.
- Lifting through full range of motion already improves flexibility.

## Cardio

- **For health:** general recommendation is about **150 to 300 min per week of moderate intensity** (or 75 to 150 min vigorous), combined with lifting on 2 or more days.
- **Intensity by the talk test:**
  - light: easy conversation;
  - moderate: can speak in sentences but not sing;
  - vigorous: only single words.
- **When:** after lifting or on another day/time. Intense cardio before heavy leg training hurts performance.
- **Types:**
  - steady moderate (incline walking, bike, elliptical, 20 to 45 min);
  - interval (e.g., 30 s hard / 90 s easy, 6 to 10 times), at most 1 to 2 times per week and never the day before heavy leg training.
- **Weight loss:** food is what drives it (dietitian); cardio and daily steps help with expenditure; lifting preserves and increases muscle mass. Encourage daily steps (e.g., 7 to 10 thousand, according to routine).
- If the person has any "yes" on the PAR-Q, vigorous cardio only with clearance.

## Sleep and recovery

- Target **7 to 9 h** per night. Little sleep drops strength, raises hunger and worsens recovery.
- Tips: regular schedule, dark and cool room, less screen time and caffeine at night, do not train heavy too close to bedtime if it disturbs sleep.
- In weeks of bad sleep, hold off on load progression and use a higher RIR.

## Menstrual cycle (optional)

Only track it if the person wants. Many people notice changes in energy, bloating, cramps and drive across the cycle; others notice nothing.
- Record how they feel in each phase, if they wish, and learn their pattern.
- On days of cramps, bloating or low energy: keep training with a slightly lighter load, higher RIR or more machine exercises; there is no need to stop if they feel well.
- Weight often rises from fluid retention on some days of the cycle; compare the average with the same period of the previous month.
- Strong or out-of-pattern symptoms are a matter for a gynecologist.

## Supplements

- **Only those prescribed by the dietitian**, at the doses the dietitian set. The agent reminds them of the times and logs whether they were taken.
- You may explain in general terms what they are (e.g., whey is milk protein powder, useful for reaching the protein target; creatine has good evidence for strength and performance), but the decision and dose belong to the dietitian or doctor.

## Motivation and consistency

- **Consistency beats perfection.** Celebrate complete weeks, records (load or reps), and measurements and photos that improved.
- Use what motivates the person (in the profile): numbers, challenges, praise, short-term goals, variety.
- **Process goals**, besides outcome goals: "train 4 times this week", "hit protein 5 days".
- Missed a session: no guilt and no lecture; adjust the week (`progression.md`).
- Show progress with data: "3 months ago your squat was 30 kg x 10; today it is 45 kg x 10".
- Now and then include an exercise the person loves, and avoid the one they hate if there is an equivalent alternative.
- If motivation drops for 2 check-ins, talk: routine, goal, boring workout, tiredness? Adjust before it becomes dropout.

## Everyday situations

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Little time today (e.g., 30 min) | do the 2 or 3 main exercises of the day, or combine into bi-sets with short rest on the isolations |
| Tired, slept badly | warm up and see how they respond; if they perform poorly, train at RIR 3 with fewer sets; sometimes resting is worth it |
| Mild cold, symptoms only above the neck | light training if they feel up to it |
| Fever, body aches, chest symptoms | do not train; rest |
| Crowded gym | swaps from `exercise-technique.md`; reorder the workout keeping compounds at the start |
| Travel or gym closed | home or hotel training (`beginners-and-splits.md`, home section), or walks; no need to compensate later |
| Special event (party, trip) | enjoy it and return to routine the next day; tell the dietitian if it is a long one |
