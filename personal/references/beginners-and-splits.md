# Beginners and training splits

The course this skill builds on says beginners use fixed reps, do not need to go to the max and respond to little stimulus, but it does not provide an entry program or ready-made splits. This file completes that with established strength-training practice.

## Who is a beginner

- Under ~6 months of regular lifting, or
- off for several months, or
- no technical mastery of the basic exercises, even if they have been to a gym before.

When in doubt, start as a beginner. The cost of starting light is small; the cost of starting heavy is injury or dropout.

## Adaptation phase (first 4 to 8 weeks)

Goal: learn technique, adapt joints and tendons, build the habit, without excessive pain.

| Variable | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Frequency | 2 to 3x per week, non-consecutive days |
| Structure | whole body every session (full body) |
| Exercises | 6 to 8 per session, one per movement pattern |
| Sets | 1 to 2 in the first 2 weeks, then 2 to 3 |
| Reps | **fixed**, 10 to 15 (e.g., 2 x 12) |
| Effort | **RIR 3 to 4** (finish a set feeling you could do 3 or 4 more) |
| Rest | 60 to 90 s |
| Tempo | controlled, lowering in ~2 to 3 s, full range without pain |
| Equipment preference | machines and guided exercises at first; free weights come in gradually with technique |

**Movement patterns per session (choose one exercise from each):**
1. Squat: leg press, box squat (sit down and stand up), goblet squat.
2. Hip hinge: hip thrust, light dumbbell Romanian deadlift, leg curl.
3. Horizontal push: machine chest press, incline push-up.
4. Horizontal pull: seated machine or cable row.
5. Vertical push: machine shoulder press (if the shoulder is pain-free).
6. Vertical pull: lat pulldown.
7. Core: plank, dead bug, side plank.
8. Optional: calves, hip abduction, light curls and triceps.

**Progression in adaptation:** when all sets are done with the fixed reps at RIR 3 to 4 with good technique, raise the load slightly. After 4 to 8 weeks, if technique is good and the client trains regularly, move to the hypertrophy phase: RM ranges, RIR 1 to 2 and a split according to available days.

### Example: beginner, 3x per week (Mon, Wed, Fri)
Workouts A and B alternate (week 1: A-B-A; week 2: B-A-B).

| Workout A | Sets x reps | Workout B | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leg press | 2 to 3 x 12 | Goblet or box squat | 2 to 3 x 12 |
| Machine chest press | 2 to 3 x 12 | Lat pulldown | 2 to 3 x 12 |
| Seated row | 2 to 3 x 12 | Machine shoulder press | 2 to 3 x 12 |
| Leg curl | 2 x 12 | Hip thrust | 2 to 3 x 12 |
| Hip abduction | 2 x 15 | Incline push-up | 2 x 10 |
| Plank | 2 x 20 to 30 s | Dead bug | 2 x 10 per side |

Rest 60 to 90 s, RIR 3 to 4. 5 min of general warm-up first.

## Splits by available days (intermediate and advanced)

Principles **(course)**: at least 1 full rest day; 48 to 72 h before training the same large muscle again; volume per session of 12 to 15 sets for a muscle trained 1x/week and 8 to 10 for 2x/week; session of 30 to 60 min.

| Days | Suggested split | Frequency of each muscle | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Full body A / Full body B | 2x | great for little time; 1 to 2 exercises per group |
| 3 | Full body A/B/C, or alternating AB (A-B-A / B-A-B) | 1.5 to 3x | full body for those who finished adaptation; upper/lower AB also works |
| 4 | Upper / Lower / Upper / Lower | 2x | very balanced; good for most people |
| 5 | Upper / Lower / Push / Pull / Legs, or a 5-day body-part split | 2x or 1x | the body-part split (1 group per day) only for advanced lifters with a lot of volume per session |
| 6 | Push / Pull / Legs x 2 | 2x | advanced lifters with good recovery; lower volume per session |

**How to fit the groups:**
- **Push:** chest, shoulders (anterior and lateral), triceps.
- **Pull:** back, rear deltoid, biceps.
- **Legs:** quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes, calves (+ core).
- **Upper:** push + pull. **Lower:** legs + core.
- The course often pairs 2 groups per workout when each muscle is trained 2x/week (e.g., chest + shoulders).

**Choosing the split:**
1. How many days the person can **actually** train, not the ideal. 3 days kept beat 5 days planned.
2. Time per session: little time calls for more days with fewer exercises, or full body.
3. Preference and priority (e.g., glutes and legs 2x per week, upper body 1 to 2x).
4. Recovery: age, sleep and physical jobs call for less volume per session and more rest.

## Home or low-equipment training

Keep the same movement patterns and swap the equipment:
- Squat: bodyweight squat, Bulgarian split squat, lunge, chair step-up.
- Hip hinge: hip thrust (single-leg to make it harder), Romanian deadlift with a backpack or water jug.
- Push: push-up (incline → floor → feet elevated), bench dips with care.
- Pull: backpack row, band or towel on a door (safely), pull-up bar if available.
- Progress through reps, slower tempo, pauses, range of motion and single-limb versions, since you cannot add kilos.
