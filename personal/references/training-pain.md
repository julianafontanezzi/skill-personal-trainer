# Training pain and discomfort

Guide for dealing with **muscular and overuse discomfort caused by lifting**: next-day soreness, the burn, cramps, a joint bothering on a given exercise. The goal is to adjust training so the person can keep training without getting worse. It is not a diagnosis; the few cases that fall outside this are at the end, in "When to stop adjusting".

Record each pain in the "Training pain log" of the client profile: where, 0 to 10 score, what it feels like, which exercise, the adjustment made and how it evolved.

## First, understand the pain (up to 4 short questions)
1. Where exactly? (in the muscle or the joint; one spot or a region)
2. Score from 0 to 10 now and during the exercise?
3. What does it feel like? (tiredness, burning, heaviness, pulling, stabbing, twinge)
4. When did it start and in which exercise or movement does it show up?

## Common types and what to do

| Type | How to recognize it | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Burn during a set** | burning in the muscle in the last reps, gone within seconds or minutes | normal, it is fatigue and metabolite buildup; follow the plan |
| **Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS)** | shows up 12 to 48 h later, diffuse in the trained muscle, hurts to press and stretch, improves with movement, gone in 2 to 4 days | normal, common after a new workout, return from a break or lots of eccentric work; train another group or do the same with light load; light movement, sleep and food help. If it always happens and is strong, volume is too high: reduce |
| **Cramp** | involuntary painful contraction, usually calf, hamstring or foot | stop, gently stretch the muscle, hydrate; usually tied to fatigue; if frequent, review volume, hydration and food with the dietitian |
| **Muscle "locked" or pulling** | feeling of tension in a muscle, no stabbing | longer warm-up, mobility, lighter load in the first sets |
| **Mild joint discomfort** | sensation in the joint (knee, shoulder, elbow, wrist, lower back), low score | adjust technique, range, grip or variation (table below) and monitor with the traffic light |
| **Sudden twinge in the muscle** | sharp pain during effort, like a "tug" | stop that exercise for the day; if the pain goes past 3, or there is bruising or loss of strength, see "When to stop adjusting" |

## Pain traffic light (during training)

| Score during the exercise | Color | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | green | continue |
| 3 to 4 | yellow | adjust (load, range, grip, variation, tempo); only continue if it does not increase during the set |
| 5 or more, or increasing | red | stop that exercise today; swap for one that does not hurt |

**Next-day rule:** if the pain the next day is worse than before the workout, the adjustment was not enough: reduce more or swap the exercise next session.

## Adjustments by region

### Lower back
- **Where it shows up:** back squat, deadlift/stiff-leg, bent-over row, leg press with hips lifting off the pad, crunch with the lower back lifting.
- **Common training causes:** losing neutral spine at the bottom of the movement, load too high for the level, tired core, range beyond control.
- **Adjustments:** lighter load and range only down to where the spine stays neutral; fill the abdomen with air before lowering; leg press with hips glued to the pad (lower less); swap bent-over row for supported rows (one-arm row, machine, chest-supported row); swap back squat for hack squat or leg press for a few weeks; add plank and dead bug.

### Shoulder (front or top)
- **Where it shows up:** bench press, overhead press, dips, heavy lateral raise, behind-the-neck pulldown.
- **Causes:** elbows fully flared on bench, loose shoulder blades, lowering beyond control, heavy load with swinging, too little back and rear-deltoid work.
- **Adjustments:** elbows at ~45 to 60°; shoulder blades together and down; dumbbells or neutral grip; reduce range (floor press); remove dips; overhead press → landmine press or light lateral raise; strengthen reverse fly and face pull; lateral raise with light load and not above shoulder line.

### Knee (front, around the kneecap)
- **Where it shows up:** squat, lunge, Bulgarian split squat, leg extension, leg press with low foot placement.
- **Causes:** leg load or volume that rose quickly, knee caving in (valgus), deep range with load, too much heavy leg extension.
- **Adjustments:** keep the knee tracking over the feet; reduce load and volume for 1 to 2 weeks; leg extension with partial range and moderate load; leg press with feet higher; lunge with a longer step and leaning trunk, or swap for step-ups; increase glute and hamstring work.

### Elbow
- **Where it shows up:** skull crushers and overhead extensions, straight-bar curls, close-grip pulls, lots of arm sets.
- **Causes:** high arm volume, straight bar forcing the wrist, heavy loads in overhead exercises.
- **Adjustments:** EZ bar, dumbbells or neutral grip (hammer, rope); swap skull crushers and overhead extensions for cable pushdown; reduce arm sets for 1 to 2 weeks; do not lock the elbow hard.

### Wrist
- **Where it shows up:** bench press, push-up, curl, back squat (grip).
- **Adjustments:** wrist aligned with the forearm (bar resting on the base of the palm, not the fingers); EZ bar; push-ups on dumbbells as handles or on fists; wider grip on the back squat.

### Neck and trapezius
- **Where it shows up:** crunch, lateral raise, rows, shrugging the shoulders without meaning to.
- **Adjustments:** hands on chest in crunches; lighter load on lateral raise with the elbow leading; shoulders away from the ears; neutral gaze (do not turn the neck to look in the mirror).

### Hip (front, groin)
- **Where it shows up:** deep leg press and squat, adductor machine with wide range.
- **Adjustments:** reduce range at the bottom; feet slightly wider with toes out; adductor with smaller opening.

### Hamstrings and calves (pulling, cramps)
- **Adjustments:** specific warm-up (light sets), smaller range on stiff-leg in the first weeks, hydration, progressive load.

## Prevention
- General warm-up + ramp-up sets before the first exercise of each group.
- Gradual progression: do not raise load and volume at the same time.
- Balance: as much pulling as pushing; glutes and hamstrings as much as quadriceps.
- Deload week every 4 to 8 weeks of hard training (`progression.md`).
- Adequate sleep and food.

## When to stop adjusting
Adjusting training is the way to handle common discomfort. Stop the exercise and advise seeing a health professional (doctor or physiotherapist) if there is:
- pain that does not improve within 1 to 2 weeks despite adjustments, or that worsens with every workout;
- visible swelling, large bruising, a pop with severe pain, loss of strength or a joint that "gives way" or locks;
- tingling, numbness or pain running down the leg or arm;
- intense generalized muscle pain with **dark urine** and weakness after a very hard workout (seek care the same day).
